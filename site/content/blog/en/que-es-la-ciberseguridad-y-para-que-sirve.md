Do you know what cybersecurity is and what it covers? At **[ISN Labs](/en/company/)** we explain why protecting your data and information systems matters in today's world.

Making sure our digital activity is secure and efficient is essential. And it affects all of us, whether we are a company or an individual.

So how can you protect your organisation's digital assets?

## **The fundamentals of cybersecurity**

It brings together the tools, technologies and practices that protect systems, devices, networks and data from digital threats.

That way unauthorised access is prevented and information stays intact. In essence, the aim is to guarantee that systems work as they should.

Cybersecurity is a shield against cyberattacks, malware and other vulnerabilities that set out to weaken our technology infrastructure.

More than that, it safeguards the reputation and the finances of companies and individuals.

## **What cybersecurity is used for**

Now that you know what "**cybersecurity**" means, it is time to explain **what cybersecurity is for** and which of its functions stand out most.

* **Protecting sensitive information**: prevents confidential company data from leaking.
* **Preventing attacks**: blocks and identifies cyberattacks before they cause real damage.
* **Guaranteeing availability**: systems keep running without interruption.
* **Legal compliance**: regulation requires cybersecurity measures that organisations and individuals have to meet.
* **Reducing financial losses**: **minimises the financial risk** associated with cyberattacks.

## **The phases of cybersecurity**

There are all kinds of **practices and strategies** for protecting digital infrastructure, and they can be grouped into four phases.

1. **Prevention**: identifying the vulnerabilities within a system.
2. **Detection**: constant monitoring to locate malicious activity.
3. **Response**: acting quickly to contain or neutralise threats.
4. **Recovery**: restoring affected systems and data to minimise the impact.

When we talk about this field, it is worth underlining that it involves both the most advanced technology and internal policies for safe use.

## **What is SIEM?**

Following on from the above, it is also worth knowing what **SIEM** means in **cybersecurity**.

SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) is a tool that collects, analyses and monitors security data in real time.

Its job is to analyse anomalous patterns and suspicious behaviour across the network.

### **Why SIEM matters**

**SIEM** brings a double advantage: it offers a complete view of the organisation's security posture and it allows threats to be detected immediately.

It also helps meet current regulation and improves incident management, thanks to the analysis of large volumes of information.

As an advanced technology, SIEM is fundamental to any modern cybersecurity strategy. There are also options such as the [Managed Cybersecurity Base Plan](/en/solutions/mssp/).

### **And my company?**

If you are wondering **what cybersecurity is for in a company**, it focuses on protecting digital assets and ensuring business continuity.

Companies put in place technical measures that boil down to **antivirus**, **firewalls** and **SIEM** systems.

They also train their staff to develop secure practices, alongside constant monitoring of systems and networks.

### **What companies gain**

There is no doubt that knowing **what cybersecurity is** and applying it inside a company translates into visible benefits.

Because risk falls considerably, suffering a direct attack becomes far less likely.

Protected systems also run more efficiently as processes are optimised, which gives customers and partners a great deal of confidence — and they value secure companies above all else.

Nor should we forget that this strategy saves money, since it avoids all the costs associated with fraud, penalties or data loss.

## **The 5 main threats**

Do you know the threats companies face? They have to protect themselves against **different kinds of digital threat** that most people are unaware of.

* **Malware**: malicious software such as viruses.
* **Phishing**: fraudulent emails or messages that try to steal information.
* **DDoS attacks** (denial of service): saturate systems until they stop working.
* **Network intrusion**: unauthorised access attempts.
* **Data theft**: leaks of confidential information.
