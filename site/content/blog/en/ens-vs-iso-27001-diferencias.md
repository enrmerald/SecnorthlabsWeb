In the current Spanish cybersecurity landscape, shaped by Royal Decree 311/2022 being fully in force and by the regulatory pressure of the European NIS2 directive, companies face a strategic dilemma: should they certify their security under the international ISO 27001 standard, or align with the National Security Framework (ENS)?

Although both frameworks share the DNA of risk management, they are neither equivalent nor applicable in the same contexts. Understanding their differences is essential to optimise investment and avoid legal risk in 2026.

If you still need an overview of the Spanish framework, you can first read our article on [what the ENS is and why it is mandatory in Spain](/en/blog/que-es-el-ens/).

## What the ENS is and what ISO 27001 is

### The National Security Framework (ENS)

The **National Security Framework (ENS)** is the Spanish regulation that is mandatory for the entire public sector and for private companies providing services to public administration, such as SaaS providers, IT providers or consultancies.

After its 2022 update, the ENS has become the national mirror of cyber resilience, requiring adequate protection not only of confidentiality, but also of the traceability and authenticity of data.

For a deeper look at when it applies and how to approach the process, read our [ENS guide: when it is mandatory and how to comply](/en/blog/ens-cuando-es-obligatorio-y-como-adaptarse/).

### The ISO/IEC 27001:2022 standard

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022** is the international reference standard for implementing an Information Security Management System (ISMS).

Unlike the ENS, adopting it is voluntary and its recognition is global, which makes it essential for companies operating in international markets or with demanding private clients.

## Main differences between the ENS and ISO 27001

Below we compare the technical and legal aspects that define each framework for 2026:

| Aspect | ENS (RD 311/2022) | ISO/IEC 27001:2022 |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Legally mandatory for the public sector and its suppliers | Voluntary international standard |
| Scope | National, Spain | Global, international |
| Approach | Prescriptive: basic, medium and high security levels | Based on risk management and PDCA continuous improvement |
| Dimensions | 5: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity and traceability | 3: confidentiality, integrity and availability |
| Controls | More than 73 measures across 3 frameworks: organisational, operational and protection | 93 controls in 4 categories: organisational, people, physical and technological |
| Audit | Mandatory every 2 years for medium and high categories | Voluntary, with annual surveillance and recertification every 3 years |

One critical practical difference lies in flexibility.

ISO 27001 allows controls to be excluded where risk analysis justifies it, through the Statement of Applicability (SoA).

The ENS, however, requires every measure of its category — basic, medium or high — to be applied in full, allowing only minimal adaptations under a strict principle of proportionality.

## Compatibility between the ENS and ISO 27001, and the value of guide CCN-STIC 825

The good news is that both frameworks are complementary.

According to guide CCN-STIC 825 from the Spanish National Cryptologic Centre, there is an official mapping confirming that having ISO 27001 in place covers roughly 70-80% of the ENS requirements.

That allows companies to adopt an **Integrated Management System**.

If you already hold ISO 27001, you only need a *gap analysis* to cover the ENS-specific measures, such as the use of qualified products or stricter requirements for time stamping and traceability.

This approach is particularly relevant for technology companies, cloud providers and SaaS platforms, where information security has to be built in by design. On that note, it can also help to review the main challenges of [cloud cybersecurity](/en/blog/ciberseguridad-en-la-nube/).

## The impact of the NIS2 directive

In 2026 you cannot talk about compliance without mentioning the NIS2 directive.

In Spain, the ENS is aligned almost entirely with the NIS2 requirements.

Certifying against the ENS therefore does more than open the door to public tenders: it also ensures that your company meets the European obligations on incident notification and risk management, avoiding penalties that can be very substantial.

Regulatory pressure does not stop at the main organisation, either. Controlling the security of suppliers, third parties and outsourced services matters more and more, especially given the rise of [supply chain attacks](/en/blog/ataques-cadena-suministro-riesgo-invisible/).

## Which one your company needs: ENS, ISO 27001 or both

The decision should follow your business strategy.

### If you want to bid for Spanish public sector contracts

The ENS is mandatory.

Not holding the Declaration or Certification of Conformity can automatically exclude a company from public tenders where that requirement appears in the specifications.

### If you operate in private or international markets

ISO 27001 is your best ally for building trust with clients in the United States, Europe or Asia.

Its international recognition lets you demonstrate that the company has a structured, audited Information Security Management System aligned with global good practice.

### If you handle health, financial or critical information

Consider the combined approach.

ISO 27001 provides the management structure, while the ENS guarantees the minimum security levels required by Spanish law.

In environments like these, continuous monitoring, early incident detection and response capability also become especially important. Solutions such as [SIEM and SOAR](/en/blog/que-es-un-siem-y-para-que-sirve-y-un-soar/) can help strengthen traceability, event correlation and the day-to-day management of security.

## Frequently asked questions about ENS vs ISO 27001

### Is the ENS mandatory for a small company?

Only if it supplies technology services or handles data belonging to public administration. That said, many large companies already require it from their suppliers in order to secure their own supply chain.

### How long do I have to comply with the new ENS (RD 311/2022)?

The official transition period ended in May 2024. As of today, every new system must comply with the new regulation and existing systems should already have migrated their certifications.

### Can I run a single audit for both standards?

Yes. Many ENAC-accredited certification bodies offer integrated audits that reduce cost, time and documentation workload for the company.

### Does ISO 27001 replace the ENS?

No. ISO 27001 and the ENS are related frameworks, but not equivalent. ISO 27001 focuses on implementing and maintaining an Information Security Management System, while the ENS sets specific requirements for systems linked to the Spanish public sector.

### Which framework is better for a technology company?

It depends on the target market.
If the company works with Spanish public administration, the ENS comes first.
If it sells to private or international clients, ISO 27001 may carry more commercial weight.
In many cases the most efficient approach is to combine both frameworks within a single GRC strategy.
