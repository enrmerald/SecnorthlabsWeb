## Is cybersecurity really that crucial?

It certainly is. Every email you send, the way passwords are handled and the systems you use at work all leave a digital trail.

**Cybersecurity matters** because it protects infrastructure and data, and keeps them from falling into the wrong hands.

Having a system hacked is one possible consequence of an attack, but there is also the integrity of medical systems, critical infrastructure and transport systems to protect.

These are all the reasons we can give you for putting in place — or improving — your company's cybersecurity policy:

### Going unprotected has consequences

If you ignore the danger and fail to implement suitable cybersecurity measures, the consequences arrive quickly.

Identity theft, financial losses, leaks of confidential data, blackmail, operations grinding to a halt, penalties from the authorities and much more.

All of those problems **add to the damage done to your business's reputation** in the eyes of customers and investors. Bear in mind that a security failure can mean lost trust and bad publicity: reputation in the digital environment is a fragile asset.

### Cybersecurity is what drives trust

As we said, people trusting your company is essential. Cybersecurity has to show up in solid policies that protect your infrastructure and convey reliability to users.

This does not only happen in private companies: you see it in government, healthcare and education bodies too.

### Attacks do not discriminate: they target small companies and large corporations alike

The popular belief is that large corporations are the main target of cyberattacks.

That is true, but it cannot hide the fact that small and medium-sized companies are vulnerable too, according to real data.

Smaller companies usually have **fewer resources to put effective defences in place** and can act as entry points into larger networks, which shows that cybersecurity is not a question of size but of commitment to security.

### Regulatory compliance

Still wondering **why cybersecurity matters**? Current regulation (GDPR, NIS2 and others) requires companies and institutions to meet a minimum standard of data protection and IT security.

As we noted above, introducing a cybersecurity strategy in your company also serves to avoid legal and financial penalties.

### Constant technological change

Cybersecurity is a discipline in constant evolution, looking for new ways to defend against attacks and to keep up with emerging attack technologies, which are also evolving continuously.

Bear in mind that factors such as artificial intelligence, remote work and heavy cloud use demand up-to-date measures and systems that can anticipate threats.
