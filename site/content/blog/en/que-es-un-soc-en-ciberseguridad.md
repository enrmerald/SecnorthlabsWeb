## What a SOC service is

SOC stands for **Security Operations Centre**.

It is a specialised team, internal or external, responsible for the **monitoring, prevention and detection of incidents** related to digital security, built around a **SIEM** tool that centralises an organisation's log management.

So it is fair to say that this service does a great deal to **protect the systems, networks and data** of any company, through **continuous monitoring**.

In the cybersecurity world it works as a command centre from which every defensive action is coordinated, while keeping IT systems confidential and available.

An operations centre is necessarily run by a team of cybersecurity specialists, like ours.

## SOC software and key technologies

A security operations centre relies on a set of specialised tools to do its job. The main ones include **SIEM**, **SOAR**, **EDR**, **firewalls**, **threat intelligence platforms** and **incident management systems**.

These tools make it possible to automate processes, centralise alerts and analyse anomalous behaviour on the network, which makes them the essential pillars of a coordinated defence.

### SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)

The **SIEM** is the central tool in a SOC's architecture. It acts as the core from which critical security information is collected, analysed and correlated across the whole technology estate.

[Read our in-depth explanation of what a SIEM is](/en/blog/que-es-un-siem-y-para-que-sirve-y-un-soar/).

### SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation and Response)

Automates incident response, orchestrates actions across different tools and cuts reaction time against threats.

### EDR/XDR (Endpoint / Extended Detection and Response)

Detect and respond to advanced threats on devices and networks, providing forensic analysis and containment.
[Here we explain what XDR is](/en/blog/que-es-un-xdr-y-como-puede-proteger-tu-empresa/).

### TIP (Threat Intelligence Platforms)

Collect, analyse and share information about emerging threats, making it possible to anticipate attacks.

### Firewalls, proxies, IDS/IPS

Perimeter defence and intrusion detection systems that block unauthorised access and analyse network traffic.

## The main functions of a SOC

A SOC is not only a surveillance centre: it carries out functions that are essential to protecting an organisation's digital infrastructure:

- **Continuous monitoring:** watches the network and systems in real time.
- **Threat detection:** identifies viruses, unauthorised access, phishing and other risks.
- **Incident response:** isolates affected systems and restores operations.
- **Forensic analysis:** establishes where the incident came from and how to prevent it.
- **Continuous improvement:** assesses how the security tooling is performing.
- **Regulatory compliance:** ensures management in line with regulations such as GDPR or ISO 27001.

## Types of SOC

There are different SOC models depending on how resources are organised:

- **In-house SOC:** run by the company's own IT team.
- **Outsourced SOC:** operated by a specialised provider such as [ISN Labs](/en/company/).
- **Hybrid SOC:** combines internal teams with external support.

SOC software applies to sectors such as healthcare, energy and transport, or to any organisation holding critical information.

## Does your company need a SOC?

Not every organisation requires a SOC, but if your company shows any of these factors, it is worth considering:

1. High exposure to cyber threats.
2. Lack of visibility or real-time control.
3. Growing complexity in the IT estate.
4. Legal or contractual requirements.
