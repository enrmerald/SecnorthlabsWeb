In a context where **94% of companies** use at least one cloud service, **cloud cybersecurity** has become a critical factor for business continuity.
On the [ISN Labs](/en/company/) blog today we cover everything you need to know to protect your data and systems in cloud environments.
Using the cloud brings **unique opportunities for scalability, efficiency and remote access**, but it also means taking on new challenges in **protection and regulatory compliance**.

## What is cloud cybersecurity?

**Cloud cybersecurity** means applying policies, processes and technologies to protect data, identities, devices and applications hosted in cloud environments.
Its goal is to guarantee privacy, access control and regulatory compliance across your digital infrastructure.

It rests on the **shared responsibility model**:
- **Provider:** secures the physical infrastructure and part of the platform.
- **Customer:** protects data, users and configuration.

📌 Knowing where the provider's responsibilities end and yours begin is essential to a secure approach.

### How these models work

- **IaaS** (AWS EC2, Azure VMs): the provider protects hardware and network; the customer manages OS, applications and access.
- **PaaS** (Google App Engine): the provider manages OS and runtime; the customer secures data and users.
- **SaaS** (Microsoft 365): the provider manages almost everything, but the customer still protects data and credentials.

⚠ **Key figure:** **82% of cloud breaches in 2023** were caused by **misconfiguration**.

## What cloud cybersecurity gives you

- **Centralised visibility**: monitor every resource, user and access from a single control panel, which speeds up detection and incident response.
- **Automatic updates and patching**: the cloud lets you apply security improvements and fixes without interrupting operations.
- **Secure scalability**: security measures grow with the business without hurting performance.
- **Simpler compliance**: cloud providers usually offer tooling to align with regulations such as GDPR or ISO 27017, reducing the administrative load.
- **Advanced data protection**: encryption, multi-factor authentication and data loss prevention built in by design.
- **Resilience against disaster**: integrated backups and recovery plans to minimise the impact of failures or attacks.

## The risks of cloud security

- **Misconfiguration** (open S3 buckets, IAM roles with excessive privileges).
- **Credential theft and phishing attacks**.
- **Lack of visibility and real-time monitoring**.
- **Using traditional tooling that was never designed for cloud environments**.

## Practices for robust cloud cybersecurity

1. **Zero Trust architecture**: continuous verification of identities and devices.
2. **MFA** and granular permission management with IAM.
3. **CSPM** and **CWPP**: tooling to monitor configuration and protect workloads.
4. **End-to-end encryption** (in transit and at rest).
5. **SIEM and SOAR** with AI for automated incident response.
6. **Active compliance**: GDPR, ISO 27017, NIST SP 800-144, ENS.
