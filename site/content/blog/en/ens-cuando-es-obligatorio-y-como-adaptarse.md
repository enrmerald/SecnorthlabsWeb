Cybersecurity has become a key requirement for any organisation operating in digital environments, and especially for those working — or wanting to work — with the public sector. If you want to understand this context better, you can also read our article on [what cybersecurity is and what it is for](/en/blog/que-es-la-ciberseguridad-y-para-que-sirve/).

In that context, the **National Security Framework (ENS)** is the reference framework in Spain for ensuring that information systems are secure, reliable and resilient.

Many companies, though, still have significant doubts:
is it mandatory in my case? Which category applies to me? What does the compliance process actually involve?

In this article we answer those questions clearly, practically and with the business in mind.

## What is the National Security Framework (ENS)?

The **ENS** is the regulation setting out the security requirements that information systems in the Spanish public sector must meet, along with those systems related to services provided to public administration.

It is currently governed by **Royal Decree 311/2022**, which adapts the framework to current environments such as cloud, mobility and remote work.

Its purpose is not only technical but strategic: to guarantee that digital services are secure, available and trustworthy, protecting both organisations and citizens.

The ENS is built on five security dimensions:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability
- Authenticity
- Traceability

Each system is assessed according to the impact an incident would have across these dimensions. The highest level obtained determines its category.

## When is the ENS mandatory?

This is where many companies misread the situation.

The ENS applies directly to the public sector, but it can also affect private companies in certain cases.

In practice, a company has to comply with the ENS when:

- It provides services to a public administration
- It develops or maintains systems used by public bodies
- It handles information within the scope of those services
- ENS compliance is required in the contract or tender specifications

In other words, not every relationship with the public sector automatically brings the ENS into play: it depends on the scope of the service or system.

It is increasingly common for the ENS to be a mandatory requirement in public tenders, which makes it a key factor in accessing new business opportunities.

## Why comply with the ENS beyond the obligation?

Although many organisations come to the ENS because of a contractual requirement, its value goes much further.

Complying with the ENS means putting in place a solid foundation of **enterprise cybersecurity**, aligned with international standards and with a comprehensive approach to protection.

Its main benefits include:

- A real improvement in system security
- Greater control over access, assets and processes
- Stronger trust with clients and partners
- A better corporate image
- Competitive advantage in tenders
- Alignment with frameworks such as **ISO 27001** or **NIS2**

Put another way: the ENS is not only compliance, it is security maturity.

## ENS categories: basic, medium and high

A key point is that ENS categories are not assigned to the company, but to each information system.

The same organisation can have systems in different categories:

### Basic category

Applies when the impact of an incident would be limited.
The controls are lighter, though still mandatory.

### Medium category

Implies a significant impact.
It requires more structured controls and more formal security management.

### High category

Applies when the impact of an incident would be serious or very serious.
It includes advanced protective measures, continuous monitoring and the capacity to respond to sophisticated attacks.

The final category is determined by the highest level reached in any of the security dimensions.

## What the ENS compliance process looks like

ENS compliance is not a one-off formality but a structured process that involves the whole organisation.

### 1. Situation analysis (gap analysis)

The current state is assessed against the ENS requirements:

- Which controls exist
- What is left to implement
- The level of maturity

### 2. System categorisation

It is determined whether the system is basic, medium or high.
This step defines the scope and demands of the project.

### 3. Adequacy plan

A roadmap is established:

- Measures to implement
- Priorities
- Resources required

### 4. Implementing the measures

This covers three kinds of action:

- Organisational: policies, roles and responsibilities
- Technical: access control, encryption, segmentation
- Operational: monitoring and incident management, supported by solutions such as [SIEM and SOAR](/en/blog/que-es-un-siem-y-para-que-sirve-y-un-soar/) or [SOC services](/en/blog/que-es-un-soc-en-ciberseguridad/)

### 5. Conformity assessment

Depending on the category:

- Basic → Declaration of Conformity (self-assessment)
- Medium/High → Certification through an accredited external audit

### 6. Continuous improvement

The ENS requires periodic review, updating of measures and constant evolution.

## Common challenges in ENS compliance

In practice, organisations tend to face several common problems:

- No up-to-date asset inventory
- No formal risk analysis
- No incident log
- Poorly defined security processes
- A one-off rather than continuous approach

These points are usually the main blockers in audits and certifications.

## The ENS within a cybersecurity strategy

One of the most common mistakes is treating the ENS as an isolated requirement.

It should in fact be integrated into a broader enterprise cybersecurity strategy.

In that context, technologies such as **SIEM, SOC or XDR** improve early incident detection, event correlation and the capacity to respond to threats, as we discuss in [XDR vs SIEM: which solution fits your business](/en/blog/xdr-vs-siem-la-solucion-para-tu-negocio/).

The ENS does not replace these solutions: it defines the framework in which they should be applied.

## Frequently asked questions about the ENS

### Is the ENS mandatory for a private company?

It can be, when the company provides services to a public administration, develops or maintains systems used by public bodies, or handles information within the scope of those services.

It can also be required explicitly in contracts, specifications or tenders.

### Is the old Royal Decree 3/2010 still valid?

No. The framework in force is governed by **Royal Decree 311/2022**, which repealed Royal Decree 3/2010.

Affected organisations therefore need to review their compliance against the current requirements.

### How long is ENS certification valid?

ENS certification is normally valid for **two calendar years**.

After that period, the certification has to be renewed through the corresponding process.

### What is the Statement of Applicability?

It is the document identifying which ENS security measures apply to the system, how they are implemented and which do not apply, justified according to the risk analysis and the context of the service.

### What is the difference between a declaration and a certification of conformity?

The **Declaration of Conformity** usually applies to basic category systems and is based on a self-assessment.

The **Certification of Conformity** applies to medium or high category systems and requires a certification body.
