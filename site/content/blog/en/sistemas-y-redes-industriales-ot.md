## What OT systems and networks are

OT (Operational Technology) systems are the systems and technologies used to control physical processes in the real world, such as those found in industrial plants, critical infrastructure, factories, electrical grids, water treatment and transport.

OT networks connect sensors, PLCs and SCADA or DCS systems, which are present in most sectors: energy, transport, logistics and many others.

While IT cybersecurity focuses on CONFIDENTIALITY, OT [managed cybersecurity](/en/solutions/mssp/) sets out to guarantee AVAILABILITY, based on physical and logical security in pursuit of operational continuity.

OT devices also use their own communication protocols, designed specifically for industrial environments. These protocols allow data to be transmitted between sensors, controllers and supervisory systems in real time, with high reliability and under demanding operating conditions. Some of the most widely used are Modbus, one of the most widespread, available in variants such as Modbus RTU (serial communication) or Modbus TCP (over IP networks); PROFIBUS and PROFINET, widely used in industrial automation, especially with Siemens equipment; BACnet, common in building control systems; DNP3, used in electrical infrastructure; and OPC-UA, which allows more standardised and interoperable communication. Many of these protocols, however, were designed without modern cybersecurity requirements in mind, which makes them a risk vector in connected OT environments.

## Differences between IT and OT networks

Before going further, we want to point out the differences between IT and OT networks.

The former manage a company's data, prioritising the integrity and confidentiality of information. They usually connect:

- Servers
- Storage
- Office equipment
- Communication systems

OT networks take an approach aimed at the stability and uptime of equipment, and of the processes it controls.

Both bring tangible benefits, but the risks cannot be ignored: industrial OT networks were originally designed to run in isolation, with no external connectivity, so most of these networks and infrastructures have no protection against external threats.

## Where the networks converge

Digital transformation in industry — driven by Industry 4.0, sensor deployment and connectivity — is bringing onto the network OT systems and devices that were originally designed to operate in isolation. That evolution, although necessary to improve efficiency, productivity and predictive maintenance, also means a considerable increase in the attack surface. Systems that used to be disconnected are now reachable and require new protection strategies.

Physical and logical network segmentation therefore becomes the first basic security measure for industrial environments. Implementing it is not straightforward, though, because numerous devices are shared between IT and OT networks, which complicates their isolation. It is common to find, for example, machine control equipment (such as HMIs or operator terminals) running only on legacy operating systems, unsupported or impossible to update, which makes them especially vulnerable targets. That equipment has to be properly isolated, and monitoring all inbound and outbound traffic to it is essential.

In that context, the right approach is not to keep IT and OT completely separate, but to move towards controlled convergence between the two networks. That allows you to make use of the value in the data industrial devices generate, to optimise processes, improve decision-making and increase competitiveness. That integration, however, must always come with specific protective mechanisms.

## The key components of an OT network

Here are the main elements that make up a modern OT architecture:

- **SCADA systems (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition):** collect real-time data and allow distributed industrial processes to be supervised and controlled.
- **DCS systems (Distributed Control Systems):** manage and distribute control of complex, continuous industrial processes.
- **PLCs (Programmable Logic Controllers) and sensors:** automate and monitor specific physical processes with high reliability.
- **HMIs (Human-Machine Interfaces):** interfaces that let operators interact with the processes controlled by the PLCs.
- **Industrial gateways:** enable communication between OT devices and IT networks, acting as control and protocol translation points.

OT networks are characterised by a robust architecture adapted to the operating environment, using specific protocols such as Modbus, Profinet or EtherNet/IP, which are very different from the usual standards in IT networks.
