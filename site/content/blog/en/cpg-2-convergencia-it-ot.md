On 11 December 2025 the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) took a significant step with the publication of the **Cybersecurity Performance Goals 2.0 (CPG 2.0)**.
After three years of operational analysis and sector feedback, this update turns what used to be recommendations into **measurable, outcome-oriented actions**.

For security leaders in environments where IT and OT converge, this is not "yet another regulation". It is a voluntary but critical roadmap, aligned with **NIST CSF 2.0**, designed to reduce real risk in infrastructure that cannot afford downtime.

## From technology to governance: the real change in CPG 2.0

The major novelty in **CPG 2.0** is the explicit addition of the **GOVERN** function.
CISA makes it clear that cybersecurity is no longer only a technical matter, but a **business responsibility**.

**Goal 1.A** requires clearly defined roles and, above all, **continuous collaboration between IT and OT teams**. That breaks with the traditional silos that still exist in sectors such as energy, manufacturing and transport.

This view connects directly with what we already covered in our article on **[industrial OT systems and networks](/en/blog/sistemas-y-redes-industriales-ot/)**.

## Protection where IT and OT collide

### Asset management and supply chain

**Goal 2.A** of CPG 2.0 insists on keeping an up-to-date inventory.
While this is usually under control in IT, the lack of visibility over **PLCs, HMIs and legacy devices** remains a structural risk in OT.

Goals **1.D (supply chain)** and **1.E (managed service providers)** further reinforce the need to assess suppliers and third parties, a critical point in regulations such as **NIS2** too.

You cannot protect what you cannot see.
The **asset inventory** is the starting point of any industrial cybersecurity strategy.

### Vulnerabilities, patching and compensating controls

CPG 2.0 accepts a common reality in OT: **you cannot always patch**.
The recent publication of critical advisories in **Siemens** and **Johnson Controls** systems, and in industrial toolkits, proves the point.

When stopping operations is not viable, CISA endorses the use of **compensating controls**, where **network segmentation** and **continuous monitoring** become the key barriers.

That approach aligns with advanced detection technologies like the ones we describe in **[NDR in OT environments](/en/blog/ndr-en-ot/)**.

## Detecting before it is too late: visibility and response

Goals **4.A** and **4.B** reinforce the need for **early detection**.
Modern threats, evasive malware and quiet lateral movement leave traditional defences short.

CPG 2.0 therefore connects directly with **24/7 monitoring** models, where services such as a **SOC-MDR** make it possible to correlate IT and OT events and tell operational faults apart from genuine incidents.

If you want to go deeper into that model, we recommend our article on **[what a SOC is in cybersecurity](/en/blog/que-es-un-soc-en-ciberseguridad/)**.

## From regulation to cyber resilience

CPG 2.0 confirms something already evident: **industrial cybersecurity** is no longer only about meeting standards, it is about **operational resilience**. Vulnerabilities will keep appearing.
The difference between a controlled alert and a crisis lies in **governance, visibility and real detection capability**.
