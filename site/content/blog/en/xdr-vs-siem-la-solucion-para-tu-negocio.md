In an environment where cyber threats keep growing, the figures are alarming: companies lost an average of more than 4 million euros per data breach last year. More worrying still, once the problem is detected, organisations need an average of 9 months to resolve it.

Against that backdrop, two solutions stand out in the enterprise cybersecurity market:

- **XDR (Extended Detection and Response):** the new generation of detection and response.
- **SIEM (Security Information and Event Management):** the established standard in security management.

Although they share similar goals, their approaches are fundamentally different. In this guide we go through the main characteristics of each one to help you pick the right fit for your case. Let's get to it.

### **SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)**

SIEM platforms have been a solid standard in **cybersecurity management for two decades.**

They are tools that collect, store and analyse event logs from multiple sources across a network, providing a centralised view of the security environment.

#### **What SIEM can do:**

- **Data collection:** consolidates data from systems, applications and devices to provide a complete picture.
- **Data analysis:** uses algorithms to correlate events and raise alerts on potentially malicious activity.
- **Regulatory compliance:** produces detailed reports that support compliance with regulatory standards.

That said, the solution needs complex management to reach its full potential, which means having qualified staff. The volume of alerts can also be overwhelming, especially for small teams.

### **XDR (Extended Detection and Response)**

XDR is a newer generation of solution designed to improve threat detection and response across multiple layers of security.

#### **Main characteristics of XDR:**

- **Advanced analytics:** reduces false positives through machine learning and artificial intelligence.
- **Coordinated response:** applies automatic or semi-automatic measures to mitigate incidents in real time.
- **Relevant alerts:** reduces workload by prioritising critical alerts.

XDR stands out for identifying threats and then acting to neutralise them, which significantly improves the efficiency of security teams.

### **Key differences between XDR and SIEM**

Although both technologies share the goal of improving visibility and responding to threats, there are fundamental differences:

- **Main focus:**
  - SIEM: centralises and analyses logs, ideal for compliance and audits.
  - XDR: focused on active threat detection and response.

- **Management and complexity:**
  - SIEM: integrates multiple data sources with a high operational load.
  - XDR: simplifies integration and reduces alert fatigue.

- **Data storage:**
  - SIEM: stores data long term, useful for audits.
  - XDR: accesses data temporarily, prioritising real-time action.

- **Response capability:**
  - SIEM: raises alerts that require manual handling.
  - XDR: automates responses, cutting reaction times.

### **Does XDR replace SIEM?**

Both solutions can coexist as complementary tools:

- **SIEM:** ideal for companies with compliance and data retention requirements.
- **XDR:** perfect for organisations looking for fast detection and response, especially with small teams.

Large companies with specific requirements can benefit from combining both technologies.

### **Choosing the solution for your business**

When deciding between XDR and SIEM, consider:

- **Choose SIEM if:**
  - You need detailed reports for audits.
  - You have a qualified team to manage alerts.
  - You want to centralise and store data long term.

- **Choose XDR if:**
  - You prioritise active response to threats.
  - You want to reduce operational complexity and false positives.
  - You have a small team and need to make it more efficient.
